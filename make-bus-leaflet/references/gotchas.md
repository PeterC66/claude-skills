# Gotchas / fixes

Reactive troubleshooting reference for the `make-bus-leaflet` workflow — read when something misbehaves. `%SK%` = the skill's `assets` folder.

## The P1 round from Peter's 32-comment review (2026-08-19)

- **A guard that only writes to stderr is not a guard.** `gen_internal.js` carried four guards on feature labels — inside the map frame, clear of the Services panel, clear of the footer plate, and *anywhere near the thing it names* — and three of them refuse to draw. Nothing captured that stream: `rollout.js` took `stderr` from `spawnSync` and threw it away **on success**, which is the one case where it matters, because a guard that refuses to draw still exits 0. Turning the capture on found **21 blocking warnings across 7 of the 13 maps** in one run, including four Peter's review had not reached. `build_log.js` now classifies every generator's stderr, writes `build-warnings.txt` into the S4 run folder beside the artwork, and stops `rollout.js --apply` before render/publish on the two classes that mean the sheet is WRONG (something refused to draw; a label names nothing) — clearing with `--force`, like the lost-label gate beside it. **Classify on the message, not the prefix** (`/not drawn/`, `/names nothing|has no geometry/`), so a guard added later inherits the right severity without anyone remembering to come back.
- **A guard should report the remedy, not only the fault — and report it about the ink the READER can see.** "It names nothing where it sits" left whoever read it to find the feature by eye on a 297×210 sheet, which is most of why six towns still carried a stranded label a day after the guard was written. It now names the nearest drawn point and samples along the ink. **Measured INSIDE the frame**, because a feature polyline does not stop at the map edge, it is *clipped* there: Huntingdon's Great Ouse runs on to y=277 on a sheet whose frame ends at 182, so the unclipped measure said 29 mm and looked survivable while the reader saw no river within reach of the words at all. The clipped measure also found what nothing else had — **March's schematic river is entirely outside the frame**, so that sheet had been printing a river name with no river under it.
- **A hand-set page-mm coordinate cannot survive a moving projection.** Every one of the seven stranded feature labels had been put on its feature by hand at some point — Ramsey's write-up even records the river name as having been "moved onto the river it names" — and then the projection, the fisheye or the frame moved. The answer is `labelPos:"auto"` (see [s3-config.md](s3-config.md)), not another constant. **The guard is still necessary**; it is what proves the placer worked, and what catches a town still on hand coordinates.
- **Where an auto-placed label runs matters less than what it displaces.** The first cut resolved `labelPos:"auto"` in the early reserve pass and put Beaconsfield's A355 on a POI symbol, because at that point neither the symbols nor the badges had claimed anything. It runs **last in the claim phase** now. Even then the reserved box costs a point label its first choice, so the scorer counts the queued anchors the box would *sit on* (`LAB.items`, added but not yet solved) and prefers a stretch with nothing waiting to be named near it — that alone recovered three POI names on Huntingdon.
- **`footer.js` was still wrapping the attribution note by CHARACTER COUNT** at `size*0.52`/char, the same estimate already found wrong in `gen_external_radial.js`'s legend and fixed there in August. It showed on the five **place external** sheets — the only ones whose note is long enough for the wrap to have to break it — where the line ran to x=240.8 mm on a band that ends at 177–195: through the "Latest version:" label and into the QR's quiet zone, on every one of the five. **Wrap to the right-hand BLOCK, not to the QR:** the address is drawn end-anchored at the same `textX1` the note wraps to, so "does not overlap" and "looks right" are different tests, and 6 mm of gutter is the difference. `font_metrics` is required lazily and defended here, same reasoning as `qr.js` — `footer.js` is vendored and a partial vendor must not take every map down for the sake of a wrap.
- **Grey is furniture, so no route may wear it.** The §5.2 river check deliberately excludes near-neutral colours ("a grey is not a river") and that exclusion left a gap: every grey on these sheets is apparatus — road skeleton, railway casing, footer type, scale bar, north arrow — so a grey *route* claims to be none of them and reads as whichever is nearest. `#BBBBBB` is the last entry of the Tol qualitative palette, where it means "other/undefined" rather than a colour, so the new guard fires exactly where a town ran to the end of the palette and took it anyway: **four towns**, of which only Wisbech's X46 was also dashed, which is what made Peter see it as a railway.
- **A hand `labelPos` on a feature is drawn START-anchored, not centred.** `drawFeatureLabel` emits no `text-anchor` unless an `overrides` label supplies one, while `labelPos:"auto"` emits `text-anchor="middle"`. Scoring a candidate spot as a centred box and then writing it as a hand `{x,y}` therefore puts the real label half its width further right — 13mm, for Beaconsfield's 25.8mm "Chiltern Main Line", which was enough to run its tail into the railway's descending bend. **Score the box that is actually drawn.**
- **When auto costs a name worth more than the placement, pin ONE sheet, not the feature.** Beaconsfield's auto rail label reserved a box in the crowded upper-left and cost "Waitrose" and "St Michael's Hall". The fix is a hand `labelPos` on the **base** `features[]` (which the geographic sheet uses) plus `labelPos:"auto"` in `internalSchematic.features.<key>` and `internalDiagram.features.<key>` — their coordinates are solved onto different grids, so one constant cannot be right on all three. Converting `internalSchematic: true` to `{"features":{…}}` is safe: the pre-stage merges `=== true ? {} : value` over its defaults, so an object carrying only `features` keeps every default.
- **The metric read the config, so a new config VALUE made it lie.** `quality_metrics.js`'s `strandedFeatureLabels` measured from `f.labelPos.x`; `labelPos:"auto"` made that `undefined`, the distance `NaN`, `best` never fell below `Infinity`, and every auto label counted as stranded — **six towns went REGRESSED on the ratchet for labels that had just been moved onto the features they name**. Reading the position off the drawn `<text>` is the fix and the general rule: this file measures the artwork. Real result after the correction: HARD 146 → 130 across 39 sheets, 11 sheets better, 4 regressed (all of them a genuine label-count trade).
- **Two-column layout changes what "rows" means in three places at once.** `design.keyCols` had to move the tier rows' start, the fare note's origin and — least obviously — `KROW_FIT`'s row count, which compresses the pitch to clear the footer plate. Feeding it the total row count instead of the rows **per column** would have kept compressing a Key that now has room to breathe.
- **`--force` is required TWICE in this round and for different reasons.** `adopt_config.js` writes a new S3, which `rollout.js` cannot see (it gates the previous S4, which still holds the old `routes.json`), so a config rollout silently does nothing without it. And March's schematic trips the new blocking-warning gate — correctly, since its river geometry really is off-frame — so the apply needs `--force` to get past a warning a human has read and accepted. Both are the gates working, not obstacles.

## Design quality / the label placer (2026-08-15)

- **`quality_metrics.js` reads the SVG a copy of `routes.json` beside it, or every ink measure silently reads far too low.** Without it, `isRouteInk()` falls back to a `width >= 2.0` rule, which misses every 1.7 mm route line — so a scratch build measured in a bare folder reported "point labels over route ink" collapsing from 13 to 0 and the ink share by ninth as `0% 0% 0% … 1% 7% 8%`, which is what an empty map looks like. The numbers were spectacular and entirely fictional. **If a measurement improves implausibly, check what the tool thinks the route palette is before believing it.**
- **The metric stamps ink from the path `d` attribute, and a clipped polyline is clipped, not trimmed.** Route tails still carry the vertices that fall outside the frame, so the raw geometry credits the sheet with ink that is never painted. It did not matter while the frame was the full 30–205 band; it mattered the moment `design.footerSafe` shortened it, because "route ink under the footer" then barely moved on sheets whose map genuinely no longer reached the band. `parseSvg` now tracks `clip-path` on the group stack and the ink loop clips each segment to the frame first. This changed the frozen Phase 0 baseline from 658 to 657, and the legend fix below took it to 628 — both called out in the scorecard, per the rule that a measurement change invalidates the comparison.
- **An external sheet's operators legend is a floating box, not a reserved column, so its own text was being counted as map labels "into the panel"** — five on St Ives, every one of them the legend's own content inside its own box. A fabricated defect is worse than no measure: `quality_metrics.js` now excludes text inside the detected legend rect, exactly as internal sheets exclude everything right of the panel edge. It surfaced because Phase 4 blocked the legend for the placer and the number did not move.
- **A label placed beyond the leader-line length cap used to be drawn anyway, without its leader.** The position was accepted by the cost function, then `_record()` silently failed the length test and set `leader = null` — leaving six High Wycombe names stranded in the left margin pointing at nothing. **A "can I do this?" test belongs in the candidate filter, not in the code that draws the result.** `_leader()` now rejects the candidate outright.
- **A terminus label's `own` exemption must match the box the cluster actually reserves, not the row it belongs to.** The frame-cut cluster reserves `bxMin-3.5 … bxMax+3.5` around ALL its badge rows *after* the per-row loop, so a label exempted only from its own row found every candidate blocked by its own badges and was dropped (St Ives' `to Boxworth`). The requests are queued during the loop and added afterwards, with the cluster's real extent as `own`.
- **Adding more candidate positions can lose labels.** A third leader ring at 3.9× the nominal gap was tried on the theory that more reach means more placements; High Wycombe lost five more names, because the extra reach let low-priority labels claim distant space that higher-value ones then could not use. Two rings only. **Measure a placer change across every town, not on the one sheet that motivated it.**
- **Taking the colour out of an icon set is not a flat recolour.** Two traps, both found by rendering rather than by reading the diff: a **pale fill is a backing plate**, so sending it to charcoal turns the allotments glyph's bed into a solid black block; and a symbol that was **already a neutral grey was drawn light on purpose** — flattening the industrial estate's `#777` to charcoal made a cluster of factories the heaviest ink on the High Wycombe sheet, which is the exact opposite of what removing colour is for. `icons.js`'s `inkify()` sends pale fills to white and keeps an already-neutral colour's tone, and only genuinely coloured symbols go to charcoal.
- **Anything drawn AFTER the labels needs to reserve its box, or it will land on one.** The north arrow is emitted at the very end of `gen_internal.js`; on High Wycombe it printed straight through route 130's terminus badge and the railway, and nothing complained because no placer had ever heard of it. Under v2 it now **places itself in the nearest blank corner** and reserves that box before the labels are solved. **Reserving it is not gate-neutral**: the first cut reserved unconditionally and immediately DIFFed two town diagrams and a place fixture, so it is v2-only.
- **The labeller's ink grid is not a map of "what is drawn" — do not reuse it for that.** It counts route ribbons and dark features, because that is what a *label* must not sit on. The river (`#9ec9e8`) is too pale to qualify, so the first auto-placed north arrow went straight into the middle of the Great Ouse on St Neots, with the search reporting the spot as 0% covered and meaning it. Anything that needs "is this square centimetre empty?" wants its own, broader stamp.
- **`if (naCfg)` vs `if (naCfg !== false)` — a default-ON feature must have a default-ON *injection*.** The schematic and diagram pre-stages compute the north bearing and inject it as an explicit `angle`, because their workspaces run at rotation 0 and cannot re-derive it. Both tested the key's **truthiness**, which was fine while every town pinned an explicit `{x,y}` — and silently wrong the moment towns started letting the engine own the arrow, because an absent key then skipped the injection and the schematic's compass pointed straight up on a map that is not north-up. Caught by comparing the drawn shaft's bearing across all three St Ives sheets before rolling out, not by any gate.
- **A config rollout without `--force` reports success and does nothing.** `rollout.js` opens by gating the town's previous S4 against the current template and returns `UP-TO-DATE` when it passes — which it does, because that S4 folder still holds the **old** `routes.json`. A freshly committed S3 is invisible to that check. The sequence is `adopt_config.js --apply` then `rollout.js --apply --force`; see `changing-the-engine.md` §2b.
- **Adding a dev tool to `assets/` does not make every town STALE, but editing a generator does.** `engine_version.js` hashes the engine files, not the whole folder, so `preview_design.js` / `adopt_config.js` / `quality_metrics.js` can be added freely. Read the hash off `status.js` rather than assuming it moved (or didn't) — a note quoting a hash from one round earlier is worse than no note.
- **`spreadIcons` needs a deterministic push direction for exactly-coincident symbols.** Two POIs at the same point have no separating vector; deriving one from the pair's loop indices keeps the output byte-reproducible, where anything hash- or random-based would break invariant 5.
- **Putting a hard-coded size behind a ternary can break every gate on a trailing zero.** Making the panel's type sizes configurable turned `font-size="3.0"` into `font-size="${PS?PS.sub:3.0}"`, and JavaScript renders the number `3.0` as `"3"` — a one-character diff that failed all 27 byte-identical gates at once with the key **absent**, which is the one case that was supposed to be untouched. The fallback has to be the string `'3.0'`. Any value whose literal form has a trailing zero, or more decimals than its numeric value needs, has this trap.
- **Equal arithmetic is not equal optical spacing when the rows are not all text.** The first panel-rhythm pass gave `Services` and `Key` the same baseline-to-cap-top gap and `Services` still looked tighter, because a route row's tallest ink is the **badge disc** (3.4 mm above the title's baseline against the cap's 2.52 mm), not its text. Space to whatever actually stands highest in the row.
- **A feature label is drawn outside the map's clip group, so a bad `labelPos` lands in the Services panel.** Wisbech shipped for months with "River Nene" struck through `46 Wisbech – March` and "A47" adrift under the Key, at `x=210` and `x=250` against a frame that ends at 196. Neither the byte gate (it is deterministic, just wrong) nor the panel-intrusion metric (it counts point labels) could see it. `drawFeatureLabel` now refuses a label right of the frame and says so on stderr, the same way the `coreBox` guard has always refused one inside the town-centre box.
- **A config key that lives inside an ARRAY needs an escape hatch in both tools — never hand-edit a committed S3 to get at it.** `--patch` / `--set` deep-merge objects and cannot reach `features[]`, `internalCorridors` or anything else in a list. `--rail` was the first escape hatch; **`--feature-pos <key>=<x>,<y>`** is the second (2026-08-15). The tempting shortcut — let `adopt_config.js` create the S3, then edit the `routes.json` it has just written — works exactly once and leaves the change unreproducible. Add the flag to `preview_design.js` **and** `adopt_config.js` together, or preview-before-adopt silently stops covering the thing you are changing.
- **A metric reporting no change is not a metric reporting no improvement.** `design.panelScale` moved every text size and every gap in the Services panel on all eight towns and `quality_metrics.js --all` stayed at 271, because every measure it has is about the **map**. Before chasing a flat number, check the tool measures the thing you changed. (`minTextMm` is the one panel-adjacent measure, and on `panelCols` towns it reports the auto-fitted **badge** text, not the body copy — so the 2.3 → 2.45 mm lift that cleared the print floor did not show there either.)
- **On a town that has been through the complexity triage, read its config before calling anything a defect** — see [complexity-triage.md](complexity-triage.md). The design-quality diagnosis called High Wycombe's shared corridor navy "an exhausted colour-blind-safe palette" and its labelled town-centre box "an empty rounded rectangle that reads as a placeholder"; both are rungs of the ladder, working as approved. `complexity.json` `applied` shows what a town has already had done to it.

- **`rollout_places.js --apply` crashed every place with `ENOENT: roads_geo.json`, even though `--` dry-run (the same script) worked fine (2026-08-10).** Root cause: `roads_geo.json`/`routes_paths.json` are S4-*generated* files (written by `build_internal_place_roads.js`'s one-time OSM pull), never registered as S2 outputs — so `stage.js pull S2` genuinely has nothing to bring them in with. The scratch/dry-run path knew this and separately copied forward every extra `*.json` from the previous S4 run; the real `--apply` path never got the equivalent step, so it silently relied on `stage.js pull S2` alone and crashed the moment it needed a file that pull never had. **A dry run passing is not proof the real apply path does the same work** — when a tool has two code paths for "preview" vs "commit," diff what each one actually copies, don't assume parity from a green dry run.
- **A footer/shared-helper file (`footer.js`) can drift on the portal side even when `status.js` says every row is "in sync" (2026-08-10).** `gen_internal.js` resolves `footer.js` via `SKILL_ASSETS` at runtime exactly like `icons.js` — but `footer.js` had never been added to the portal vendoring-drift table (`changing-the-engine.md` §4 / `status.js`'s `portalDrift()`), so a footer-only change (dropping the printed engine version) silently left the portal's copy stale. It didn't show up as a "DRIFTED" row — it just wasn't checked at all. It surfaced instead as a **2-byte SVG mismatch** in `npm run verify:area` (`Map v6.23 · …` from the old footer vs `Valid from …` from the new one) — small enough to look like noise, but `verify:area` doesn't pass on noise. **Any file `gen_internal.js` resolves via `SKILL_ASSETS` needs a row in the portal vendoring table, not just the files that "feel like" the engine** (`icons.js`, `render.js`, the generators themselves) — added, so this can't recur silently.
- **A delivered map's pre-flight verify can fail on the live host even though it passes locally, and that's the live host telling you the truth, not a flake (2026-08-10).** `npm run deliver` re-verifies against the host's *own* running container before touching anything — if the host is running older code than what produced your local fixture (because `git push` never auto-deploys there — see [[project_bus_portal_go_live]]), the pre-flight step correctly fails with the exact same "old footer text" mismatch `verify:area` would show locally on stale code. The fix is to deploy (`git pull && docker compose build portal && docker compose up -d portal` on the host — `docs/DEPLOY.md` §9), not to touch the map data. The script did exactly what it's for: it left the live service completely untouched and told you why.

- **Converting a busway town's `external[]` to radial: drop the leading town-name stop.** The busway `leftBranch()` loop draws `stops[1..n-1]` (skips index 0), so busway-shaped data conventionally starts each arm's `stops` with the town's own name as a throwaway index-0 placeholder. `gen_external_radial.js`'s spoke loop draws **all** of `stops[0..n-1]` as ticks — it has no such skip — so copying busway `stops` arrays straight into a radial config draws a spurious "St Ives" (etc.) tick right next to the hub on every spoke, badly cluttering a multi-spoke town. Strip the leading town-name entry first (confirmed against March's radial config, which never carries one). **Real-corridor spokes will still look crowded even after that fix** if several services share a real corridor before diverging (St Ives's A/B/5A busway trio all run Fenstanton→Swavesey→Longstanton→Oakington→Histon before splitting to three different termini) — that's the same congestion the busway generator's shared "spine" was built to absorb, so expect to spread those spokes' bearings **20–30°+** apart (not the true ~10° geographic gap) and set `side` on each to steer their intermediate labels onto different sides of the line, same remedy as any crowded radial town.
- **A radial `bearing` near 270–300° can swing the hub-ward end of a long town name straight into the top-left operators legend / arm-note text**, because at those bearings `dx,dy` both go negative (up-and-left) and the terminus townNode has no collision awareness of the legend block. Nudge the bearing back toward due-west (~275–285°) rather than pushing it further round; the legend's own position (`legendAt`) is the other lever if a town genuinely needs a spoke through that quadrant.
- **2026-08-06: `gen_external_radial.js` gained four always-on fixes** (badge clearance, legend backing box, note word-wrap, version placement), applied by rolling out to all 8 built towns after an external-map audit found every non-High-Wycombe town had at least one of these problems (High Wycombe alone had hand-tuned `badgeOffset`/`legendAt.box`, which is what made it look "right" while every other town relied on defaults that don't hold up once a town gets busy). Specifics:
  - **Badge clearance is now automatic.** `_boff` (mm back from the terminus for the first badge) is `Math.max(badgeOffset ?? 8, measureNodeWidth(label,timeLabel)/2 + 4.5)` — it always clears the terminus lozenge's own computed half-width, so a wide destination name (e.g. "Hemel Hempstead") no longer needs a per-town `badgeOffset` override to stop the green box painting over the badge (badges are drawn before the box, so anything shorter than the box's half-width got covered).
  - **The operators-legend backing panel is now always drawn**, auto-sized to its content (badges + operator names + the wrapped note), not opt-in via `legendAt.box` any more. An explicit `legendAt.box` still wins (hand-tuning escape hatch) and also **caps the note's wrap width to the box's own interior** — needed because without a cap the note-wrap step (below) would size lines past a hand-tuned box.
  - **The "runs as two arms" note now word-wraps** (`wrapText()`, no 2-line cap unlike the label `wrap()` helper) instead of one unbounded `<text>`. Wrap width defaults to `max(contentWidth, 100mm)` — a **wide-but-short** wrap is preferred over narrow-but-tall, because the auto panel's HEIGHT is what risks reaching down into a nearby terminus lozenge (St Ives: a 60mm floor wrapped its 3-multi-arm note to 6 lines, reaching low enough to cover the Hinchingbrooke box; 100mm wraps the same note to 4). Don't push the floor much past ~100mm either — St Ives' note at a 110mm floor wrapped to 3 lines but widened the panel enough to newly collide with the Ramsey-via-Old-Hurst lozenge on the *right*; there's a real width/height trade-off here, not a knob to crank freely.
  - **The version stamp moved from mid-right-edge (rotated -90°) to the bottom-right corner (horizontal, unrotated)** — the old spot is exactly where a busy town's easterly/southerly spokes terminate.
  - **Auto-sizing isn't a substitute for the "spread bearings, only then place the legend box" sequence in s3-config.md.** Two towns still needed a manual nudge after the auto-box shipped: **Beaconsfield** (`legendAt:{x:10,y:155}` — the default top-left position put the panel right under the High-Wycombe-bound spoke's terminus, which the box then painted over) and **Wisbech** (`legendWrap:{perRow:2}` + explicit `legendAt.box:{w:80,h:75}` — 6 operators including two long names made the auto width alone reach ~107mm, which was wide enough to fully swallow the "Sutton St James" lozenge in the top-left quadrant; legendWrap folded the 4-badge Stagecoach-East-Midlands row onto 2 rows to bring the content width down, and the explicit box then also capped the note's wrap width per the point above). **Always check the panel's actual rendered `<rect>` (x/y/width/height) against every nearby terminus's box, not just eyeball it** — hand math on `measureNodeWidth`/`measureText` is a rough (deliberately generous) estimate and was off by enough in both cases above to miss a real overlap.
  - **`rollout.js`'s real-apply path had a bug that silently dropped `internal-schematic.svg`/`internal-diagram.svg`**: it ran `schematize_internal.js`/`diagram_internal.js` straight out of the new S4 dir without ever copying them in first (unlike its own dry-run scratch path, which does copy them), so the spawn failed with ENOENT, `r.ok` was never checked, and the output just vanished from the commit with no error surfaced. Caught when High Wycombe, Beaconsfield, St Neots and St Ives all lost their diagram (St Ives also its schematic) across a `rollout.js --all --apply`. Fixed by copying the file before `runNode` and checking `r.ok`, same as the dry-run path already did. **If a rollout ever reports DONE for a town with `internalSchematic`/`internalDiagram` configured, re-check `status.js`'s Schematic/Diagram columns afterward** — a silent-fail here won't show up any other way.
- **2026-08-06 (later same day): the legend backing panel could drift away from its own content when `legendWrap` was in play — root cause, not just a stale box.** User feedback: Wisbech and High Wycombe's box outline didn't line up with the "Operators & services" text; Ramsey's box overwrote the Peterborough terminus. Two separate bugs, both in `gen_external_radial.js`'s legend block:
  - **The real bug (Wisbech/High Wycombe): `ly` gets reassigned after the `legendWrap` loop** (`ly = yy - 6.6*OPS.length`, to keep the arm-note's default y-offset sane), but the backing `<rect>`'s own y-position (`ly-10`) was computed from this *same, already-mutated* `ly` — so once a town used `legendWrap`, the panel's top no longer matched where the header (`Operators & services`, drawn earlier from the *original* `ly`) actually sat. The panel would land too low, floating away from its header and clipping its own last operator row / note. **Fixed by capturing `const legendTopY = ly` before any mutation and using that for the rect's y (and for the auto-height calc)** — a town that never uses `legendWrap` is unaffected (ly is never mutated there), so this is byte-identical for every non-wrapped town.
  - **The masking symptom (both towns): a frozen `legendAt.box:{w,h}` doesn't get revisited when routes are added.** Wisbech's `box:{w:80,h:75}` and High Wycombe's `box:{w:92,h:76}` were both hand-set against an earlier, shorter operator list; by 2026-08-06 the actual content needed more room in both cases, so the frozen height clipped content regardless of the `ly` bug. **`gen_external_radial.js` now lets `box.w`/`box.h` be set independently** — the missing dimension auto-sizes — so a width hand-tuned to dodge a spoke label (High Wycombe keeps `box:{w:92}` to stop route 40's "Stokenchurch" being swallowed) no longer freezes the height too.
  - **Fixing the panel's height then exposed a second, pre-existing overlap at High Wycombe**: with the panel correctly sized (taller than its old, too-short frozen height), its bottom edge reached the "Oxford" terminus lozenge (bearing 272°, nearly due-west, landing almost level with the hub). Old fixed-y-position bug had *fully* swallowed that lozenge in translucent white (bad, but at least not a half-cut edge); correctly-positioned auto-height clipped just its top ~6mm (worse-looking, a hard line through the box). Fixed by nudging the spoke's `bearing` 272°→267° to drop the terminus ~11mm further south, clear of the panel. **Any time a legend panel's size or position changes, re-check bearings near 260–290° (the same quadrant as the panel) for newly-exposed or newly-created overlaps — don't assume the panel is the only thing that moved.**
  - **Ramsey: a single wide operator row, not a box or `ly` bug.** One operator (`Dews Coaches`) with 8 routes and no `legendWrap` drew all 8 badges on one row, auto-sizing the panel to ~90mm wide — wide enough to reach under the Peterborough terminus (bearing 326°, landing top-right of the panel). Fixed with `legendWrap:{perRow:4}`, same remedy as Wisbech's original Stagecoach-East-Midlands row, just not yet applied since Ramsey is a Tier-2 unattended draft (`draft_town.py`) that had never had its external layout hand-checked — see `DRAFT-REVIEW.md` in Ramsey's town folder.
  - **Takeaway for any future legend collision**: check the rendered `<rect>` in the SVG (x/y/width/height) against (a) its own content's actual draw coordinates, not just against nearby termini — a panel that's internally misaligned will pass an eyeball check against its neighbours while still clipping its own text.
- **Wisbech's Manea/March/March/Chatteris spokes (bearings 183/196/207/214) landed within a 31° arc, all forced onto the RECT's bottom edge, with gaps far below the boxes' combined half-widths** — the classic crowded-radial symptom from `s3-config.md`'s "spread bearings by hand" sequence, just not yet applied to that cluster. Re-spread (175/197/215/228 — see Wisbech's 2026-08-06 S3 commit) using the rect-projection math in `gen_external_radial.js`'s `rayToRect()`: compute each candidate bearing's terminus `(tx,ty)`, then require adjacent gaps ≥ sum of half box-widths (`measureNodeWidth(label)/2`) + ~2mm margin. Don't eyeball spacing in degrees — the same angular gap compresses hugely near a rect corner vs. the middle of an edge.

- **An all-or-nothing "is this line a duplicate?" test lets a 90 %-parallel siding survive whole** (same session) → the first `railMerge` dropped a polyline only if **every** point lay within tolerance of one already kept, so a track that runs alongside the main line and then diverges at one end failed the test and was drawn in full, re-doubling the very line the pass existed to de-duplicate. Sampling matters too — coverage judged at OSM vertices alone is wrong when the diagram engine has left 100 mm segments, so the line is densified to `tol/3` first and the collinear padding removed afterwards. **Lesson: for geometry de-duplication, the useful question is "which parts of this are new?", not "is this new?".**
- **The "content buried under the footer" metric only counted text STRADDLING the plate edge, so text sitting wholly inside the band scored zero** (`quality_metrics.js`, found 2026-08-15 while checking the railway-weight rollout) → the test required `b.y1 > footerTop && b.y0 < footerTop + 6 && t.y < footerTop + 2.5`, i.e. a window around the top edge. Anything placed a few mm lower was invisible to it *and* invisible on the sheet: six river/canal/railway labels at `y=196..200`, the "Only main stops…" diagram note on three towns, and a route badge at `y=197.7`. The scorecard read **"content buried under the footer: 12 sheets → 0"** while seven real ones were sitting there. The first fix — dropping the bounds and excluding the footer's own lines by their opening words — was worse: the external sheets' second note begins "Confirm live times…", so every external footer was reported as a buried label. The rule that actually works is **document order**: `footer.js` paints the plate and then its own text, so anything emitted *before* the plate is map content the plate is about to bury. **Lesson, and this is the fourth of its shape in this project: a metric that reports zero is not evidence of zero — check what it would have to see to report one.** Fixing it moved the tracked total 271 → 276 before any of the day's fixes were applied.
- **A terminus badge CLUSTER was clamped by its centre, not its height — the exact twin of the 2026-07-28 row-width bug, in the other axis** (found 2026-08-15) → the horizontal clamp had already been widened to `rowHalf` for multi-badge rows, and the vertical one was left at a flat `MY1-3.4`. But a cluster with several distinct "to X" texts draws one row per group at `by ± (n-1)/2 × RH`, so on Huntingdon a four-group cluster put route 9's badge at `y=197.7` against a frame ending at `192.2` — **5.5 mm inside the footer plate**, painted and then covered, so route 9 appeared in the Services panel and nowhere on the map. Nothing complained: badges are drawn outside the map's clip group, the byte gate only proves determinism, and the footer-text metric skipped badge digits because they carry `dominant-baseline="central"`. **Lesson: when you fix a size-dependent clamp in one axis, fix it in the other the same day** — the 2026-07-28 entry above even records the general form of this, and the y-axis half still sat there for three weeks.
- **A feature label sited outside the frame is drawn anyway, and there are three edges it can escape through, not two** (2026-08-15) → `drawFeatureLabel` already refused a `labelPos` inside `coreBox` and one right of the frame (the Wisbech "River Nene" case). It had no test for *below* the frame, and six sheets were shipping one: Huntingdon's "River Great Ouse", March's "River Nene (old course)", Ramsey's "Canal", St Neots' "East Coast Main Line" on both its sheets. Feature labels are drawn outside the map's clip group, so `design.footerSafe` — which shortens the frame — does not help them at all. March's was worse than a config mistake: it came from the engine's own **legacy fallback** for a town with no `features[]`, whose `labelPos` has always been `{x:40,y:200}`, fine against the old flat frame bottom of 205 and 4.8 mm inside the plate once `footerSafe` moved it to 192.16. The guard and the new default are both gated on `design.footerSafe`, so the five place sheets stay byte-identical until the Phase 8 re-vendor. **Lesson: an invariant worth enforcing on one edge is worth enumerating all the edges for.**
- **"Put a scale bar on the geographic sheets" — except none of them is to a single scale** (2026-08-15, building plan §4.6) → every town runs the radial fisheye in `compress()`: true scale inside `internalRoads.focus.coreKm`, then `focus.comp` beyond it. Across all eight towns `comp` is **0.30 to 0.50**, so the page scale steps by **2× to 3.3×** at the core boundary, and two towns carry a detail lens as well. A plain bar would have been right in the middle of the sheet and wrong by 3× at the edges — the difference between a fifteen-minute walk and a forty-five-minute one — and nothing in the build would have complained, because a scale bar is just a line and a number. The plan item was written by someone (me, the session before) who had read the drawing code and not the projection code. **Lesson: before drawing anything that makes a quantitative claim about the map, go and read what the projection actually does to distance.** What saved the device was a measurement rather than an assumption: the true-scale core is 34–71 mm in radius on a 190 × 162 mm frame, so it covers most of the *page* even though most of the *content* is outside it — which is what makes a bar captioned `town centre scale` honest and useful rather than a fudge.
- **The schematic workspace sets `focus:{comp:1}`, so a derived sheet looks perfectly uniform to the code that draws on it** (same session) → `schematize_internal.js` and `diagram_internal.js` hand `gen_internal.js` coordinates that have been solved onto a tube-map grid; distance is gone, but the *projection* the workspace declares is the identity, so any measurement `gen_internal.js` makes of its own page scale comes out clean and confident and means nothing. This is why `notToScale` is a flag the pre-stages set rather than something the engine infers. **Lesson: a pre-stage that rewrites the input can leave the downstream code with a perfectly consistent picture of a world that no longer exists** — when one stage synthesises coordinates for another, it owns telling the second stage what they are not.
- **The map was fitted to stops it does not draw, and the fit set asked the wrong question** (Ramsey, found 2026-08-15 doing plan §4.2) → under `internalRoads` the fit set is "core stops", selected by **ATCO prefix** — i.e. by which parish a stop is in. That is not the same question as *does this map draw anything there*: the route line comes from the matched road graph, and where the graph ends the line ends, so a served stop beyond that end sits in the fit with no ink anywhere near it. Ramsey had six on X31 out at Ramsey St Mary's, **2.7–3.6 km** from any drawn line. They stretched the fit box from 75 mm wide to 141 mm, so the town was drawn 8 % smaller than it needed to be and shoved into the right two-thirds of the frame — the entire left column measured **0.0 % route ink**. The sheet looked like a composition problem and was a projection one. Fixed as `fitMaxOffPath` (default 1500 m, and the measured separation is wide: six towns' worst core stop is ≤ 79 m, High Wycombe's is 929 m *by design*, Ramsey's six start at 2,701 m). **Lesson: when a sheet looks badly composed, check what the fit is computed from before rearranging anything** — and beware any set whose membership comes from an administrative code rather than from what the code actually drew.
- **`emptyNinths` had never been acted on, so nobody had checked it against a sheet that looks FINE** (2026-08-15) → it counts ninths of the frame with essentially no *route ink*. On an internal sheet the frame starts below the title and excludes the panel, so that is fair. On an **external** sheet there is no frame, so it measured from y=0 — counting the whole title band as empty map — and the operators box, which is composition and not route ink, made its column read bare. March external looks well balanced and scored 3. Both corrected; `share` (the 3×3 fractions) is now in `--json`, because "4 empty ninths" is not actionable without knowing which four. **Lesson: a warn-only metric nobody has acted on has never really been tested** — the first time you use one to justify a change, re-validate it against a good sheet as well as a bad one, or you will "fix" something that was right.
- **Two agents in one working tree is fine for editing and not fine for ROLLING OUT** (2026-08-15) → a second session was editing `gen_internal.js` (the `design.badgeFit` follow-up) while an 8-town `rollout.js --all` was running. Each town's build copies the generators fresh from `%SK%` at S4 time, so the eight towns picked up **five different snapshots of the file** and recorded five different engine hashes into their manifests. Nothing broke — the other session's feature was gated off and inert, so every sheet's output was identical and all 27 gates passed — but the provenance stamps are now wrong, and the only clue is `status.js` showing towns on hashes that no single commit contains. **The rollout is the step that freezes a hash into 31 manifests, so it wants a quiet tree.** Re-stamped 2026-08-16 — all 8 towns are on one hash again, and the two rollouts that day were both run into a quiet tree, which is the practice to copy. Related: `preview_design.js` writes to `assets/design-preview/` when `--out` is omitted, and a `git add` over the skill directory will sweep 3 MB of 300 dpi JPGs into the repo — now gitignored.

## Text that does not fit its shape (2026-08-16)

- **The bug is WIDTH, not character count — do not "fix" it with `size / len(text)`.** `design.badgeFit` was reported as "four-character route keys overflow the badge". Measured, the worst offenders were **three** characters: High Wycombe's `WW1` is 5.87 mm of Arial Bold in a 4.8 mm disc, against `301S`'s 5.61 mm. All-caps codes (`WW1` `LGW` `LHR` `OXF` `M40`) run far wider per character than digits, which are all 0.5562 em. A length-based rule would have missed five real cases and wrongly widened `X31`, which fits. **Ask `font_metrics.js`; never divide by string length.** The same applies to `measureNodeWidth` and the hub — see `design-quality.md` under `badgeFit` / `hubFit`.
- **Fit the shape to the text, not the text to the shape, once the type is near the legibility floor.** Shrinking a badge's font to fit its disc is the smaller change and it fails where it matters most: `301S` inside a 2.4 mm-radius stop badge needs **1.8 mm** type, under the 2.4 mm floor `quality_metrics.js` enforces. Hence the stadium.
- **Express a widened pitch as `<old literal> + <extra>`, never recompute it.** Every clamp, pitch and reserve box downstream of `badgeHalfW` is written that way, so a town with no wide key adds a floating zero and stays *bit-for-bit* identical rather than merely close. `2.6 + 0.2` is `2.8000000000000003` in JS; `x - 2.8 - 0` is `x - 2.8`. This is the difference between the byte gate passing and a day spent on a 1-byte diff.
- **A label's `own` box is its EXEMPTION, so widening it with the symbol makes things worse.** The terminus cluster's `reserve` box must grow with a wider badge row; its `own` box must not. Widened, Ramsey's `to St Ives` and `to Huntingdon` both came inside and printed over the ribbon (3 → 5 defects); left alone, one keeps a clean spot (3 → 4) and neither is dropped, because both are `mustPlace`.
- **`quality_metrics.js` cannot see this class of bug at all**, so do not read its total as a verdict on a fitting change. Badge glyphs are skipped by `t.central`, and nothing measures the inside of a symbol — `badgeFit` and `hubFit` together moved the 31-sheet total by −1 while fixing every overflow on the board. Render and look, and run the overflow check below.
- **Two traps in writing that overflow check**, both of which produced confident, wrong answers first time: (1) a bare `width="([0-9.]+)"` regex also matches **`stroke-width`**, which reported 1,375 of 1,376 lines overflowing a "0.70 mm" shape; (2) walking forward from a shape over consecutive `dominant-baseline="central"` text picks up **neighbours**, not just contents — the operator legend draws a badge and then the operator's name beside it, which yielded 49 confident false positives. Filter on `text-anchor="middle"` **and** on the text's `x` matching the shape's centre.
- **Check the whole class once you have found one instance.** The hub-node defect was invisible until the badge fix prompted a sweep of every shape-contained text on all 31 sheets. That sweep also proved the terminus lozenges did *not* need changing (tightest 0.88 mm a side across 312 lines), which saved moving every lozenge and every spoke badge offset to fix nothing.

## The spider's fan (2026-08-16)

- **Moving the spokes moves the TERMINI, and the sheet has furniture that does not move with them.** Ramsey's `design.spokeSpread` rotated the Peterborough spoke 7° and its terminus lozenge landed under the operators legend — which is drawn after the spokes, with an opaque backing panel, so the node simply vanished. Nothing complained: the byte gate proves determinism, and `quality_metrics.js` has no measure for "a node is under the legend". **After any composition change on a spider, look at the legend, the note block and the stamp** — they are pinned in page coordinates and the spokes are not. The remedy is `legendAt`, which exists for exactly this; the freed space from spreading is usually where it wants to go (Ramsey's went to the now-empty left-middle).
- **Check whether two spokes are literally the same before treating a crowded fan as a layout problem.** Ramsey's 303 and 305 had **identical `stops` arrays**, the same destination and the same journey time, drawn as two parallel spokes with two `Huntingdon` lozenges side by side. That is not a composition defect and no amount of bearing arithmetic fixes it — `external[].routes:["303","305"]` merges them, and the sheet went 3 defects → 0 on that change alone. The de-duplicator had been quietly hiding half of it: with the two spokes 30 mm apart it dropped the *duplicate* stop labels, so each spoke showed a different half of the same four villages and neither looked wrong enough to investigate.
- **An even spread is a claim about direction, so clamp it.** The spread is capped at `maxShift` (default 30°) from the true bearing precisely so a spoke can be nudged to the edge of its compass sector but never into the opposite one. Without a cap, four spokes at 49/170/190/326 would have gone to a perfect X and Huntingdon — SSW of Ramsey — would have been drawn pointing SW by 39°. The cap is the difference between a diagram and a wrong map.

## The panel, the corridor rule and the water (2026-08-16)

- **Do not pass a `--patch` / `--set` JSON containing en-dashes or middots through PowerShell 5.1's argv.** `preview_design.js` and `adopt_config.js` take their JSON as a command-line argument, and PowerShell converts it to the system ANSI codepage on the way to `node.exe`, so `Mon–Sat` and ` · ` arrive mangled — and they arrive mangled *silently*, straight into a committed S3. Every panel string in this system has both characters in it. Write the JSON to a **UTF-8 file** and spawn the tool from a tiny Node runner (`JSON.parse` the file, pass the re-stringified value), because `child_process` hands argv to Windows as UTF-16. The same applies to any commit message or note with typographic punctuation.
- **A defect the metric cannot see is not a defect you can skip rendering.** `design.panelCorridors` rebuilt the entire Services panel of two sheets and moved the 31-sheet total by **zero**, exactly as `panelScale` did before it, because no measure in `quality_metrics.js` looks at the panel. The only evidence that the work was right is the rendered panel and the label-set diff — and the label-set diff is the one to read carefully here: it lists 14 strings lost and 13 gained, and the question it answers is not "did the count go up" but **"is every service's destination still printed somewhere"**.
- **Regrouping rows is a chance to drop information silently.** Six services sharing one road through town have six different destinations beyond it, and a naive "one row per lane" writes one destination and loses five. That is why `corridorDesc` takes as many subtitle lines as the lane needs rather than a fixed `[title, subtitle]` pair — and why the check after the change is the *old* panel's content, not the new panel's tidiness.
- **A stack of badges in a panel column has to take its own line, and the arithmetic says so before the render does.** Six 5.2 mm discs are 34 mm across; a `panelCols` column is 49 mm and the title needs ~40 of it. The tidy-looking alternative — one fixed badge column wide enough for the widest stack — leaves every single-badge row floating 13 mm from its title. Measure the widest stack against the column before choosing a layout.
- **Pick a threshold from what it means, then look at the data — not the other way round.** Judging whether a `corridorPalette` group earns its shared hue needed a bar. "Half, because below that most of each line is going somewhere else" is an argument that survives the answer; the three High Wycombe groups then measured 0.55, 0.58 and 0.79, two of them within 0.05 of the line. Had the numbers been read first, any bar chosen would have been a rationalisation.
- **A warning is only worth adding if somebody then reads the build output.** The §5.2 check found March's X32 on the river's cyan and printed it on every build for two rollouts while the session believed a *scan script* that said two towns were affected. The scan read `routes.json` `features[]`; **March has none** and draws its river through `gen_internal.js`'s legacy `river_geo.json` fallback, which the engine's own check sees because it reads the *built* feature list. Two lessons in one: **a second implementation of a rule is a second chance to get it wrong** — prefer running the engine and reading its stderr over re-deriving the rule in a scratch script — and **grep the rollout output for the warnings you have just added**, because a rollout prints a lot and a new warning looks exactly like the old noise.
- **Plain colour distance flags a grey as a river.** The §5.2 check (route drawn in the water's colour) uses CIE76 dE **and** a hue test, with near-neutrals excluded on chroma: `#BBBBBB` sits 21.3 from the `#9ec9e8` river purely on lightness, and a grey line is not mistakable for water, while the real offender `#66CCEE` is 14.6 away *and* 8° of hue from it. A single-number colour test on a palette that contains greys will always fire on the wrong thing.
- **Score a replacement hue against every colour on the sheet, then render it anyway.** Ramsey's X31 came out of the scoring with teal as a strong second (worst separation 39.1), and teal's worst neighbour was the green 303 — which happens to run *beside* X31 for the length of Wood Lane. "Runs beside" is not in the numbers. Indigo (49.3) was the right answer and the render is what confirmed it.

## The legend, and what a metric cannot see (2026-08-16)

- **A measure that cannot express a defect will certify it.** Previewing `design.spokeSpread` across the seven remaining towns buried **62 pieces of artwork** — whole spokes, their destination lozenges, route lines — under the operators legend on **six of them**, and it was written up as "adopt on six" because every defect number improved. `quality_metrics.js` measures labels and ink *on the map*; the legend is furniture, so content vanishing beneath it was free. Peter caught it by looking at the sheets. **The tell was in the data and went unread: the towns that clashed worst were the towns whose numbers improved most**, which is not the shape a real improvement has. When a change makes every number better at once, ask what the numbers do not cover *before* writing it up.
- **The gap was closed in both directions, and that is the pattern to copy.** A fix to the artwork (`design.legendPlace`) *and* a fix to the measure (`symbolsUnderLegend`, `routeLinesUnderLegend`). Fixing only the artwork leaves the next composition change free to do it again unseen; fixing only the measure leaves you with a red number and no remedy. The two were built independently and then agreed on the same single occlusion on the shipped board — which is the reason to trust either.
- **Rank two harms before you combine them into one score.** The placer's first cut minimised a single weighted "how much is covered" number and parked High Wycombe's 92×80 mm legend squarely **on the hub**, because a hub covers less area than the three spokes it had been covering. Burying a *named place* and dimming a *stroke* differ in kind, not degree: symbols became a hard constraint and route ink the objective within it. **A weighted sum quietly buys the unacceptable thing whenever it happens to be small enough.**
- **"No good answer" is a result, and the tool should say it rather than pick the least bad one.** Four towns have legends (78–106 mm wide) that cannot be placed clear once their spokes spread. The placer leaves those where they are and names the real remedy — *shrink the legend* — because moving a box to a differently-bad spot costs the reader the one thing they had, which is knowing where the legend lives between versions.
- **Check that the warning you are told to read can actually reach you.** `preview_design.js` forwarded `gen_internal.js`'s stderr and **discarded the external generator's on success**, for its whole life. Every warning `gen_external_radial.js` prints — the water-palette check of the entry above, and now the legend placer — was invisible in a preview. The 2026-08-16 rule "run the engine and read its stderr" was being defeated by the tool it was meant to be read through.
- **A detector with a convenience clause has a blind spot with a shape.** `quality_metrics.js` found the legend with `r.y0 < H * 0.5`, so it had **never once seen Beaconsfield's legend**, which sits at y=145 — the one town that parks it in the busy half of the page, and therefore the one town where legend-aware measures mattered most. Match on the thing's actual signature (here: `fill-opacity="0.94"` with a `#ccc` hairline) rather than on where it usually is.
- **The bus skills are a junction, and the outer repo ignores them.** `~/.claude/skills/make-bus-leaflet` is a Windows junction to `C:\u3a St Ives\.claude\skills\make-bus-leaflet`, and `~/.claude/skills/.gitignore` lists `/make-bus-leaflet/`. `git status` run *inside* the junction reports against the inner repo (`claude-skills`) and looks entirely normal; `git add` run from the outer repo refuses with "paths are ignored by one of your .gitignore files". Commit engine work from `C:\u3a St Ives\.claude\skills`.

## The icon grid, and a design rule that measurement refused (2026-08-16)

- **The box a symbol is given and the box it draws in were never the same.** `gen_internal.js` declares `POI_HALF = 2.1`, blocks out a 4.2 mm square per POI and holds centres `iconMinSep` apart — while the shipped glyphs reached **±2.48 mm**, about 18% outside it. So two symbols the placer scored as clear could touch, and a label placed hard against the box edge could still land on ink. It had been true since the first icon was drawn and no measure could see it, because every measure asks about the *drawn* ink and the placer asks about the *declared* box; nothing compared the two. **When a module declares an extent, check that the artwork obeys it** — the redrawn set maps its 20-unit live area onto that box exactly, and doing so was where most of the 80 → 44 improvement in labels-over-a-foreign-symbol came from.
- **A casing must be one pass over the whole glyph, never `paint-order:stroke` per path.** Per-path casing lets a later part of the same drawing eat an earlier one: the tree's trunk cut a white notch out of its own canopy. Draw a fattened white silhouette of everything first, then the glyph on top.
- **Do not pass `{ density: 300 }` to sharp for these SVGs.** The generators put `width="3508" height="2480"` on the root, so sharp already rasterises an A4 sheet at exactly 300 dpi. Adding a density makes it **14617 px** wide, every mm→px conversion is then out by 4.17×, and a crop comes back as a blank corner of the page with no error. Use `%SK%\crop_compare.js`, which gets this right and exists so nobody writes the conversion again.
- **`design-preview/` had never been tracked and was never ignored.** Every `git add -A` in the Buses repo after a preview staged 30-odd scratch SVGs; previous sessions must have unstaged them by hand each time. Now in `.gitignore`. **Check what `git add -A` actually staged** before committing in a repo that a tool writes into.
- **Restricting an optimiser costs exactly what the optimiser was optimising, and that can be the right answer or the wrong one.** Plan §2.5 wanted one fixed design for off-map continuations. Every restriction of the label placer made the sheets measurably worse — no leaders +5, inboard half only +15, straight inboard +21 — because the four "inconsistent" arrangements were the placer routing around ink, not variation for its own sake. **The premise of a plan item can be wrong, and building the thing is how you find out.** Two facts of the geometry decided it: inboard along the line is *on the ribbon*, since the line runs past the badge to the frame; and the clearest space by a frame cut is *outboard*, the one direction a device cannot use. Built, measured, documented, left off.
- **When the numbers and the picture agree, stop.** On this project they have disagreed twice (the panel, the legend) and the habit of doubting the metric is correct — but §2.5's +15 was confirmed by the 300 dpi crop, and at that point the answer is the answer. Doubt the measure when the picture contradicts it, not when it says something unwelcome.
- **A GATED engine change makes the whole board read STALE, and it is not eight jobs.** Adding a key that defaults to today's behaviour still moves the engine template hash, so `status.js` shows every town behind it — and `/bus-work` reads that as a worklist of stale renders. The gate has already proved the sheets regenerate byte-identically, which is the exact claim the stamp makes, so correct it with `engine_version.js --stamp <latest S4>/routes.json` per town rather than re-rendering and bumping eight versions for no pixel change. **Only ever after the gate says PASS** — a town that DIFFs means the change was not inert after all.

## What two printed sheets found that nine sessions of measurement did not (2026-08-16)

Peter printed **two** sheets at 300 dpi for the plan's §5.3 palette check — High Wycombe internal, St Ives internal, borderless, unscaled. Seven defects came back, none of them expressible by any measure in `quality_metrics.js`, and each turned out to be a whole category rather than a one-off. The measures for all seven took one session to write. **The cheapest instrument in this project is a printer, and it had not been used since before Phase 0.**

- **Doubting your measurements is not the same as finding your blind spots.** Five sharpenings of `quality_metrics.js` came from asking why a number would not move (four) or why one was already zero (one). That is a real skill and it was useless here: every one of these seven was a question nobody had asked, so there was no number to doubt. **A sheet that scores well on every question you asked is not a sheet without defects.**
- **A metric that counts only what is on the page can be improved by putting less on it.** `labeller.js` has written `unplaced.json` beside every sheet since §1.9 shipped in Phase 2 — **94 dropped labels board-wide**, none of them counted, while both the scorecard and the tool went on repeating Phase 0's "silently dropped labels are not measurable" for four more sessions. **A "not measurable" note has a shelf life and nobody checks it.** When a phase adds an artefact, the same commit either reads it or says in the consumer's comments what would change that. This is the legend lesson with the sign flipped: that measure could not see what was *covered*, this one could not see what was never *drawn*.
- **Report drops as a RATE, not a count.** High Wycombe sheds 52 of 130 candidates (40%) and that is substantially rungs 2 and 2b of the triage working as designed; St Ives sheds 2 of 44 (5%). The raw count makes the biggest town look like the worst engineering. What the threshold should be is a product judgement, not a placer one — see `complexity-triage.md` before gating it.
- **A defect can have two venues.** Two route hues are compared **on the map** where their lines run together, and **in the panel** where every badge sits beside every other regardless of where the routes go. They are different pairs: High Wycombe's `30`/`32`/`36` reds — the ones Peter actually saw — never run within 6 mm of each other, while `29/32/32A` vs `40/130` do. Measuring only the map venue would have reported his own example as clean.
- **Right conclusion, wrong number, is the failure that survives review.** Matching a feature to its drawn ink by *Lab-nearest colour* picked a `#33383d` used 69 times for other furniture over the chequer railway's own `#4a4a4a` casing, because it is nearer to the `railway` type default of `#333333`. High Wycombe's "Chiltern Main Line" measured **115 mm** from the wrong ink instead of **78 mm** from the right ink — stranded either way, so nothing looked wrong. **Exact match first, nearest only as a fallback**, and when a new measure agrees with the conclusion you already held, check its number as hard as you would check a surprise.
- **A measure applied to a sheet that cannot exhibit it fabricates defects.** `features[]` belongs to the town, but the radial spider draws no river and no railway, so the first run of the feature-label check reported every feature on every external sheet as stranded. Gate a measure on the sheet type that can actually show the thing.
- **Ask what a device NAMES before making it honest about something else.** The scale bar was made honest about the fisheye (§4.6) and captioned `town centre scale`. Nobody asked whether the town centre is drawn: on High Wycombe `coreBox` deliberately replaces it with a labelled box, so the caption now names the one region of the sheet with no roads on it.
- **A sheet's own index must not promise what the map does not draw.** St Ives badges `VL14` in the Services panel under its own operator heading, with a description — and `#BBBBBB` appears as a stroke nowhere on the sheet. St Neots does the same with `69`. Telling a reader to look for a line that is not there is worse than omitting the service.
- **Print margins are a systemic property, so measure them as one.** Every sheet puts its footer credit **3 mm** from the right trim and its last line 4 mm from the bottom; borderless printing over-scales 2–3% to guarantee bleed, which on A4 is ~3 mm, so what survives is about 1 mm. Peter saw it on St Ives and not on High Wycombe **and the two are byte-for-byte identical in this respect** — at that margin, whether a sheet looks right is down to printer feed tolerance. Six sheets are tighter still; the worst is a panel line at **1.54 mm**. Warn at the 5 mm conventional floor, fail below the systemic 3 mm, or one engine fix fails all 31 rows at once and the verdict column stops meaning anything.
- **Check where a route key is actually RENDERED before citing it as an example.** `design-quality.md` headlined `WW1` (5.87 mm of type in a 4.8 mm disc) as `badgeFit`'s worst case; on that sheet `WW1` only ever appears inside a prose map note. Measured type widths are not evidence that anything on the sheet is that wide.
- **An SVG cannot be measured without the JSON that sits beside it, and nothing will tell you.** `quality_metrics.js` reads `routes.json` from the sheet's own `ci-reference` folder to learn which colours are route ink. Extract only the `.svg` files from an old revision — as `contact_sheet.js` did on its first run — and every route-ink measure silently collapses: the 2026-08-15 baseline scored **89 point-labels-over-ink instead of 244** and **500 defects instead of 655**, with no error and a perfectly plausible table. Extract the whole directory, and sanity-check an old-side total against the frozen baseline before believing any delta computed from it. Related: **comparing across a gap in time compares two versions of the tool as well** — measure both sides with today's one and say so on the artefact.
- **Adding ink to fix a margin defect can create a margin defect.** `design.printSafe` shipped a longer scale-bar caption (`scale outside the town centre box`) and a longer panel subtitle (`· not shown on this map`), and both landed outside the 5 mm margin the key exists to enforce — High Wycombe's caption **0.42 mm off the page**. Neither showed in the preview: the first needs the `coreBox` caption *and* a left-edge placement, the second a subtitle already near the limit. **Measure the rolled-out sheets, not only the preview**, and when a change adds text, check that text against the rule the change is enforcing.
- **A reserved footprint must describe where the text is ANCHORED, not merely how wide it is.** The scale bar's box grew rightwards from the bar's *left end*; the caption is drawn **centred on the bar's midpoint**, so a caption wider than the bar sticks out both sides. Invisible for as long as the caption was about as wide as the bar. The width was also a character-count estimate while `font_metrics.js` sat in the same folder — **never estimate a width by counting characters in this engine.**
- **When two implementations of one rule disagree, ask which one can EXPRESS the defect.** `gen_internal.js` finds a panel-only service from `TRIM[route]`, keyed by route; `quality_metrics.js` finds it from the SVG, which can only ask by *colour* — so a route sharing a colour with a drawn one is invisible to it. The build finds Ramsey's `301X`; the metric reports Ramsey clean. The newer implementation was the right one, which is the opposite of the usual lesson and just as important.
- **Gate a FLOOR, not only ceilings.** Every quality measure here counts something wrong that is *on* the page, so a placer that drops a label to avoid a collision scores better for dropping it. `quality_gate.js` therefore gates `mapLabels` as a minimum as well as `HARD`/`drop` as maxima — `drop` alone does not close the hole, because a config change can remove a POI outright with the placer never reporting a drop.
- **A gated key can hide an UNGATED change, and every byte gate will still pass.** `design.printSafe` corrected the scale bar's reserved footprint *without a gate*, and that correction moves the device: rebuilt with the key removed, **High Wycombe and Ramsey differ** (the other six do not). All 27 gates passed throughout, because every committed S3 carries the key — so the gate only ever exercised one side of the branch. **When a key is on every town, the ungated path has no test unless you write one**: copy each town's `ci-reference`, delete the key from `routes.json`, and build with the old and new engine side by side. And a bug fix is not an exemption from invariant 2 — it is a change like any other.
- **A gate nobody can fail is not a gate.** `status.js`'s `--json` branch called `process.exit(0)` unconditionally while the human-readable branch exited 1, and `--json` is the form `.github/workflows/gates.yml` runs as its gating step — so CI was green regardless of the result, on both repos, for as long as that workflow had existed. Three lines to fix; noticing was the work. Same shape as `preview_design.js` swallowing the external generator's stderr on success for its entire life. **Check that the thing you are told is gating you can actually return a failure.**
- **A deliberate open drift needs a closing date, or it silences the report it lives in.** The four DRIFTED portal-vendoring rows were correct, documented and expected for three weeks while the design-quality plan held the hand-off back — and for those three weeks a row of expected red made an *unexpected* red indistinguishable. It is also why the `--json` exit code could not be fixed first: flipping it would have turned CI red for a state everyone knew about, and taught them to ignore CI.

## Dashed lines, and the fix that only landed in the copy (2026-08-17)

- **A round line cap plus a dash pattern whose gap is narrower than the stroke draws a solid line, not a dashed one.** `stroke-linecap="round"` adds `width/2` of ink beyond *each* end of every dash. The external generators drew limited-service spokes at `stroke-width="3.4"` with `stroke-dasharray="1.6 2.2"`, so each 1.6 mm dash became 1.6+3.4 = **5.0 mm** of ink and each 2.2 mm gap became 2.2−3.4 = **−1.2 mm** — i.e. adjacent dashes overlapped. St Ives's VL14, 9v and 301o printed as one scalloped grey caterpillar; the only visual trace of the intended dash was the bumpy edge. The fix is a **butt** cap whenever the path is dashed, plus a gap comfortably wider than the stroke (`2.6 2.4`). **Rule of thumb: with round caps, the usable gap is `gap − width`; if that is not positive the pattern is decorative only.** The same arithmetic already governs `railStitch`/chequer (which is why the chequer used butt caps from the start) and the `canal` feature default (`3 1.6` at w2.4, latent — no town has a canal yet, and it would have fused the first time one did).
- **The reason it came back is that the fix was made in the fork, not the source.** `line()` exists in *three* generators — `gen_external_busway.js`, `gen_external_radial.js` and `gen_external_places.js`. The places copy is literally headed `// ---- primitives (from gen_external_radial.js)`. That copy hit this exact defect on Beaconsfield 380 and St Neots 66, was fixed there on 2026-08-06 — in the commit titled *"Bring the place external map up to the area engine's level"* — and the two generators it had been copied **from** were left as they were. Every area external map kept the defect; it simply stopped being *visible*, because St Ives is the only area town with `limited: true` spokes and nothing rebuilt it for eleven days. **When you fix a defect in a primitive that was copied from somewhere, fix the original in the same commit, and say so in a comment in all copies** — all three now carry one, naming the other two.
- **The gate to write for a change like this is "did anything OTHER than the intended thing move?"** A/B the town's *own vendored generator* against a patched copy of it, on every build on disk, and require every differing line to contain `stroke-dasharray`. That reported 7 area towns byte-identical, St Ives exactly 3 changed paths, 0 regressions. It is also falsifiable, which is the point: forcing `cap = 'butt'` unconditionally — the obvious wrong way to write this fix, and one that would have blunted the end of every solid route on every sheet — turns it red on 8 builds. A gate for a rendering change that cannot distinguish "the dashes changed" from "every line ending changed" is not measuring the fix.

## A field name is a claim, and this one was false (2026-08-17)

Found while benchmarking against TfL / NCT / Cerović ([`publisher-benchmark-plan_2026-08-17.md`](../../../../u3a%20St%20Ives/Using%20AI/Buses/Development%20Docs/publisher-benchmark-plan_2026-08-17.md) in the Buses repo). The plan's headline recommendation — encode service frequency in the line work, as every surveyed publisher does — was about to be built on `gtfs-services.json`'s `tripsAtTownPerWeekSample`. **Peter said verify it first. It failed.**

- **Counting rows in GTFS `trips.txt` is not a rate, and never was.** A journey that runs Mon–Fri is **one** trip record whose `service_id` carries five day-flags; the day expansion lives in `calendar`/`calendar_dates`, not in the trip. `COUNT(DISTINCT trip_id)` therefore returns *timetable patterns*. The old name claimed a rate **and** a sample and was wrong in both halves — it is now `tripPatternsAtTown`, and `journeysPerWeek` beside it is the real thing, expanded over actual dates.
- **The undercount was ×2.0 to ×5.7, and the multiplier is set by somebody else's data modelling.** A route filed as one Mon–Fri calendar scales by five; the same service filed as three day-type calendars scales by under three. It is a choice inside the operator's GTFS export, so **no constant can correct it** — which is exactly why "it's only a scale factor, the ranking still holds" is the tempting wrong answer.
- **It got the ranking wrong in all four towns checked.** St Ives put the market-day `9` sixth of eight, above two routes that run twice as often. **Wisbech swapped its two busiest routes** — the top of the table, where a *frequent* tier would be drawn. Huntingdon reported `400` ahead of `X2` when the two are level. **Drawing a false claim is worse than drawing nothing**: a reader can discount a map that says nothing about frequency and cannot discount one that says something wrong.
- **Verifying the number produced a DESIGN finding the number could not.** St Ives' `300` runs 5 journeys, all 09:50–13:50 (a shopping bus); the `69` runs 4, at 07:00/07:10 and 17:30/18:10 (a commuter shuttle). They rank adjacent on any count and are opposite products. **Span matters as much as count in a market town**, which is the respect in which this problem is not Nottingham's — an urban network's frequent routes also run all day. Hence `firstDeparture`/`lastDeparture` in the output, and why the tier model is still an open question rather than "three tiers, like NCT".
- **Two implementations, written separately, agreeing is the reason to trust either.** The standalone checker (`Development Docs\verify_frequency_2026-08-17.py`, kept as a permanent second opinion) and the ported `gtfs_query.py` reproduce each other exactly, to the minute on the spans. Keep it that way: when these numbers are next doubted, run the checker rather than re-deriving trust from the tool under suspicion.
- **The range immediately earned its keep, and it was free.** Sampling 12 consecutive weeks rather than one shows route 101 as `(0–6)` — it vanishes entirely in term time. That is the seasonal Sat/Sun excursion the S1 manifest had recorded **by hand** on 2026-08-10, rediscovered automatically by a tool that knew nothing about it. **One week is a point estimate of a thing that varies by school term; sample consecutive weeks so the window straddles a boundary.**
- **Prove a change is inert structurally, not by observing green gates.** All 27 gates passed after this, which on its own means little — `gtfs-services.json` is an *input file* the generators never read, and `engine_version.js` hashes only `gen_internal.js`, `gen_external_radial.js`, `gen_external_busway.js` and `icons.js`. A data-stage tool **cannot** move a sheet, and knowing why is worth more than watching PASS scroll past.
- **Before renaming a field in a shipped JSON, grep for it and check how readers copy.** Nothing consumed it by name: `draft_town.py` spreads the record with `{**s}` (so a rename flows through harmlessly) and reads only `days`; the portal stores the file but never parses that key. **A `{**s}` pass-through is what makes a rename cheap — and also what would have let a silently wrong value propagate for ever.**
- **Fixing the misleading name is part of the fix.** Leaving `tripsAtTownPerWeekSample` in place "for compatibility" would have preserved the exact hazard — a plausible number that no caller can use correctly. The related `SVCF` trap (the active-service filter is opt-in, so expired timetables are counted) was deliberately **left alone and made irrelevant instead**: the new measure counts every journey against a real date, so it is immune by construction, and switching the legacy filter on by default would change `days`/`validFrom`/`termini` for existing towns — fields that *do* feed `draft_town.py`. **Make the new path correct; do not smuggle a behavioural change to the old one into the same commit.**

## An honest field can still be the wrong thing to draw from (2026-08-17, later the same day)

The tier-model decision that followed the frequency fix above ([`frequency-tier-model_2026-08-17.md`](../../../../u3a%20St%20Ives/Using%20AI/Buses/Development%20Docs/frequency-tier-model_2026-08-17.md) in the Buses repo, measured by `measure_frequency_tiers_2026-08-17.py` beside it). The previous lesson was a field whose *name* lied. This one is the harder case: `journeysPerWeek` says exactly what it means, and drawing tiers from it would still have been wrong.

- **A weekly total is a volume; a line weight has to express availability.** Tiering the 78 drawn lanes on `journeysPerWeek` misplaces **10 of them, in both directions**: Wisbech's `66` (85/wk) runs every 30 minutes and would be drawn below routes with three times the journeys, while St Ives' `301` (48/wk) and March's `X32` (46/wk) clear any sensible threshold while having 4¾- and 6⅓-hour holes in the middle of the day. Volume rises with route length and operating hours, neither of which is what a passenger is reading the line for.
- **`firstDeparture`/`lastDeparture` cannot see a hole, so span is the wrong second measure.** St Ives' `69` — the commuter shuttle whose contrast with the `300` motivated the whole span idea — spans **07:00–18:10**, wider than the 5A's working day. The thing that makes it a commuter shuttle is the ten-hour gap between its second and third journeys. Use **the largest gap inside 07:00–19:00** for the pattern and the **median gap across 09:00–15:00** for the frequency; keep the two endpoints, but never tier from them alone.
- **Use the median gap, not the worst.** The first pass measured frequency by the largest gap of the day and demoted nearly every turn-up-and-go route on the board, because the first two buses of the morning are typically an hour apart at 05:00.
- **The tier belongs to the DRAWN LANE, not the route.** `internalCorridors` bundles co-running services into one line — High Wycombe's `102` lane carries `102, 103, 104, 105, M40, X74`, whose merged timetable is **2,684 journeys a week** against members ranging from 138 to 822. Merging is easy (union the departure lists, recompute the gaps); the catch is that `gen_internal.js` resolves corridor membership **per segment**, so a corridor lane has no single frequency and a single weight would be true of the trunk and false at the branches. Corridor towns (High Wycombe, Ramsey) should sit out a first frequency rollout for that reason alone.
- **A lower median over non-zero weeks turns a bank-holiday service into a weekly rate.** High Wycombe's `130`, `300` and `WW1` each have exactly one `service_id`, **no `calendar` row**, and only added dates — August bank holiday, Easter, May Day, plus Christmas for `WW1`. `journeysPerWeek` reports them at 24, 24 and 8. `journeysPerWeekRange` shows `(0–24)`, so it is visible if you look, but **any measure derived from the headline number needs a weeks-active floor**. They are drawn on the sheet today, which is a separate question worth asking: either they run four days a year, or their everyday timetable is not in this feed.
- **`direction_id` is not safe to split a small service by.** Splitting the `69`'s four journeys by direction leaves two, and a per-direction reading describes it as an evening-only shuttle. Take gaps across both directions for the pattern; use the busier direction only for the headway.
- **Test a classifier by asking whether any sheet collapses to a single class.** Tiering on headway alone (turn-up-and-go / hourly / worse) is the obvious model and it puts **every Ramsey lane in the bottom class** — nothing there beats two-hourly — so the whole sheet would print dashed, which reads as *this town has no buses* rather than as information. Widening the middle class to take in the all-day two-hourly routes is both the fix and the honest description of a market town.
- **Read the exit code of the thing you ran, not of the pipe you ran it through.** `node status.js | tail -25; echo $?` reports **`tail`'s** status, which is 0 whatever node did — so "status.js exit 0" went into two documents and a commit message while `status.js` was in fact exiting **1** the whole time, on the two DRIFTED portal-vendoring rows (`status.js:224` folds `driftRows.some(r => r.same === false)` into its `bad` flag). Everything else on the board really was passing, which is exactly what makes it survivable and hard to spot. Redirect to a file and check `$?`, or use `PIPESTATUS`. Same family as the `--json` branch that called `process.exit(0)` unconditionally: **a status you never saw go red is not evidence.**
- **A headway of 0 minutes is the feed telling you a journey is in it twice.** High Wycombe's M40 registers every journey up to **four** times — same route_id, same headsign, same 18-stop sequence, different `service_id`, two of them live on any given weekday — which dragged its median core-day headway to 0. **12 routes on the board repeat a departure minute**, six materially: Wisbech A/B/C at exactly ×2.0, M40 ×1.68, St Neots 905 ×1.7, High Wycombe 20 ×1.49, both X74s ×1.4. That means **`journeysPerWeek` is inflated by up to double for those routes** — St Neots' 905 reads as the busiest lane on its sheet at 431/week and is an hourly service. Every count in `gtfs_query.py` is therefore of distinct JOURNEYS, where a journey is `(departure minute at the town, direction, full stop sequence)`, and the collapsed records are reported as `typicalDayDuplicates`. **Compare the whole stop sequence, not the endpoints**: an innocent cause gives the same signature on small routes — St Ives' 5A has two trips leaving Bar Hill at 11:40 in the same direction, of 41 and 42 stops, to different termini — and a first-and-last-stop signature would wrongly collapse those. **Count the duplicates on the DAY, not across the feed**: St Ives' B has 60 trip records that duplicate another on paper under calendars that never coincide, so nothing about it was ever inflated, and a feed-wide count would have reported a problem that does not exist.
- **When a derived number is impossible, stop and look at the rows.** The 0-minute headway was the only reason any of the above was found, and it surfaced *after* 565 field comparisons between two implementations had come back clean — because both implementations were making the same assumption about the data. **Agreement between implementations tests the code, not the feed.** The sanity check that catches this class is asking whether each number is one the real world can produce.
- **`strftime` per (trip, date) was the most expensive thing this module did.** `_runs()` formatted the date inside the innermost loop — 265,000 calls and 1.8 s of High Wycombe's runtime. It now takes the pre-formatted `YYYYMMDD` and the weekday column name, computed once per day by a caller that loops days outside trips, which left the de-duplicated version faster on the small towns than the original was. Anything expanding a calendar over 12 weeks × 8 towns wants the same shape.
- **A line weight is a label-placement budget.** `design.frequencyTiers` at 2.4 mm for the frequent tier cost three real place labels across the board — *The Highcrest*, *The Hive*, *Bushmead Primary* — because a heavier route line is a bigger obstacle to the placer. 2.2 mm recovers two of the three at no visible cost to the hierarchy. `quality_gate.js` gates `mapLabels` as a **floor** for exactly this: set the width from the labels, never accept the labels lost for the width. Below about 2.3 mm the placer's result stops changing at all, so there is no point fine-tuning past it.
- **Only key what the town actually has.** The frequency Key emits a row per tier *in use on that sheet*. Ramsey and March have no frequent lane; a Key line for a class the town has none of is a false claim about the network, and it is the same defect class as drawing a line style with no Key row at all.
- **Judging a line-weight change needs the whole sheet, not a crop.** The dashed limited tier looked reasonable in a 40 mm crop and was obviously wrong at page scale, where 40% of a market town's lanes being dashed makes the network read as provisional — worst on Ramsey, which has no frequent lane to anchor it. `crop_compare.js` settles detail; a full-sheet pair settles whether the sheet still reads.

## A symbol that renders is not a symbol that scans (2026-08-18)

Building `qr.js` for `design.sheetQr` turned up three things, and only the first is about QR codes.

**Transposing the format-information block produces a plausible symbol that never scans.** The 15 bits go down column 8 and along row 8; write them as `m[8][i]` where you meant `m[i][8]` and the finders, timing, alignment and data are all symmetric enough to survive it. The picture looks exactly like a QR code. **Every one of 35 test matrices failed to decode**, and the failure said nothing about which of the six or seven stages was wrong. What found it in one step was a **reverse decoder written independently on the Python side** — read both format copies, check they agree and satisfy the BCH code, un-mask, walk the placement order, de-interleave, require zero Reed-Solomon syndromes. Two implementations of the same spec agreeing is a much stronger claim than one implementation plus a picture, and it localises the fault instead of just reporting it.

**A conformance test can certify something a phone will refuse.** With the spec-minimum four-module quiet zone, one of 78 realistic BusMaps URLs (Beaconsfield Waitrose at level Q) could not be **located** by OpenCV at any scale or any size — `detect()` returned false, so it never got as far as decoding. The symbol was perfectly valid: the reverse decoder read it back fine. Six modules of quiet zone found all 78. The spec minimum is a floor for a conforming reader, not a margin for a real one, and the only reason this was caught is that the check ran on **many** payloads rather than one.

**Judge it in the medium it ships in.** The gate that actually matters is decoding the code out of the rendered 300 dpi JPG, which is what a council prints — not out of a matrix dumped from the encoder. It passes on all four sheet types, whole-page and cropped. Downscaling that JPG says how much camera it needs: readable photographed at 1600 px across the A4, not at 1200. Both controls were checked red first — corrupt the modules past the EC budget, or shift them one column, and the decode must fail.

## An optional page device should decline rather than bury (2026-08-18)

`design.legendPlace`'s rule for "no position on this sheet is clear" is *leave it where it is and warn*, because a sheet with no legend is not a sheet. `design.howToUse` reuses the same search and needed the **opposite** ending. First try on Huntingdon, the panel found nowhere, stayed at its default bottom-left, and printed "find where you want to go, around the edge of the diagram" directly on top of the **St Neots** terminus lozenge — a help panel covering up the thing it is telling the reader to look for. Not drawing it costs a town a nicety; drawing it there costs a reader a destination, with nothing on the sheet to say it was ever there.

So the two rules differ by whether the device is **mandatory**, and it is worth asking that question of any new one: the legend gets first pick of the clear ground and stays put when there is none; the panel searches what is left and omits itself, warning on stderr and naming both remedies. A fully-specified `at:{x,y}` is a decision rather than a preference, so it switches the search off — otherwise a town could pin the panel and still get nothing.

Two corollaries. **A page device's height is what collides**, not its width, because the spokes fan out horizontally — the first cut used a 74 mm column and made an 81 × 72 mm panel that nothing could clear; 92 mm gives 99 × 49 mm and three towns can place it. `buildLegend`'s note wrap learned this on 2026-08-06 and the lesson did not transfer, because it was written as a comment about that note rather than as a rule about furniture. And **the defect count is not the measure here**: with the panel omitted on five towns their external sheets are unchanged, which reads as "no benefit" in the numbers and is exactly right on the page.

## A config key can cost you page, not just ink (2026-08-18)

`design.sheetUrl`/`sheetQr` raise the footer's backing plate, `design.footerSafe` ends the map frame above that plate, so a footer key silently **shrinks the map**. On the internal sheet the plate top moves 193.6 → 185.7 mm and dense towns lose labels to it (High Wycombe 80 → 77, Beaconsfield 40 → 38, St Ives 41 → 38) while sparse ones lose none. `mapLabels` is a ratchet **floor** precisely so this cannot pass unnoticed.

The instructive part is the split: about 6 mm of that 7.9 mm is the printed **URL line** and only 1.9 mm the code itself, so the obvious economy — shrink the QR — buys almost nothing, and shrinking it below 0.40 mm per module would cost a scan to save a millimetre. Measure which half of a feature is expensive before optimising the half you happened to think of. Same shape of mistake as the print-safe margin, which was fixed at the baseline before anyone measured the descender.

`gen_external_radial.js` had to hoist its footer notes and options to the top of the file for this: the plate top must be known **before** any page furniture is placed, and one options object is now passed to both `footerPlateTop()` and `footerBand()`, so the plate the furniture is fitted around cannot differ from the plate that gets drawn. `gen_internal.js` has always done this; the external sheet only needed it once the footer could grow.

## The footer-plate lesson above was still only half learned (2026-08-18, later the same day)

Adopting `sheetUrl`/`sheetQr` for real, on all 13 maps, found the terminus lozenges on the external sheet had **not** been following `FRAME_Y1` the way the note above says every page device must — `RECT.y1 = 182` was a **hardcoded number standing in for "just above the plate"**, true only by coincidence while the plate happened to sit near there. Growing the plate 5.5 mm (195.2 → 189.7 mm, this session's URL+QR) put St Ives' bottom row — Hilton & Elsworth, Boxworth, Trumpington P&R, Bar Hill — **6 mm inside** the now-taller opaque band, silently deleting all four journey times. The lesson from the internal-sheet fix ("the plate top must be known before any furniture is placed") is not itself sufficient: every boundary DERIVED from the plate top has to actually be re-derived, not left as a constant that was correct on the day someone measured it. Fixed by making `RECT.y1` follow `FRAME_Y1` and having `townNode()` additionally clamp the drawn box, so an `overrides.branches.*.terminus` pin is covered too, not just the default ray-to-frame geometry.

**And `quality_metrics.js` scored the buried sheet as an improvement — +8 labels — while this was happening.** The `howToUse` panel's bullets are `<text>` nodes and count; a `<rect>` lozenge painted then covered by an opaque plate is neither present nor absent to a tool that only reads text elements, so the metric had nothing wrong to report. **This is the second time this project has learned that a defect can be invisible to every gate it has and visible in the first two seconds of looking at the JPG** — the first was the buried-artwork class `design.legendPlace` exists to prevent (see "An optional page device should decline rather than bury", above; `spokeSpread` buried 62 pieces of artwork across six towns while every defect metric went **down**). Both times the fix was the same shape: stop trusting a metric that cannot see the failure mode and go look at the rendered sheet. Before shipping any change that touches page layout, render at least one dense sheet and look at it — don't reason from the numbers alone, however green.

Two smaller findings from the same rollout, worth having on file:

- **A measurement function used for two different jobs can be wrong for both at once, in opposite directions.** `gen_external_radial.js`'s `measureText()` was `length * size * 0.58` — a character-count guess, described in its own comment as "deliberately erring wide so the panel never clips." It was used to size the legend's backing panel AND to pick the arm-note's wrap width. Real Arial prose runs ~0.50em, so the panel came out ~16% wider than its own content, and the SAME 16% error made the note wrap ~16% *narrower* than the panel it was sizing — the two errors compounding into a dead column measured at 7–24% of panel width across the eight towns before the fix (St Neots worst, 25.5 of 105.6 mm), reported by Peter as "lots of space on the right... and it seems to do new lines unnecessarily." `howToUse`'s panel already measured on `FONT.textWidth()` for exactly this reason — the fix was bringing the legend into line with a rule the codebase had already learned once, in the same file, and not applied to its older neighbour.
- **A width-discipline fix applied to one panel branch does not reach the others, and `gen_internal.js` has four ways to draw the Services panel** (plain single-column, `panelCols` multi-column, `panelGroups` grouped-by-operator, `panelCorridors`). Only the corridor branch measured its subtitle against the column width before this session; the code's own comment already said so ("the plain panel row has no width discipline at all"). St Ives (grouped) and every plain/multi-column town shipped a subtitle running off the page the first time a tier phrase made one long enough to overflow — not a new bug, a bug that had no trigger until frequency tiers supplied one. **When a fix belongs to "the Services panel," check how many functions draw a Services panel** before considering it applied everywhere; `grep -n "font-size=\"\${PS?PS.sub` or similar across the file, not just the branch you're looking at, would have caught the grouped-branch miss immediately instead of after a second real-sheet render.
- **A per-tier Key row count can outgrow a fixed row pitch that had never needed to flex before.** `KROW` (the Key's row height) was a plain constant; adding one row per drawn frequency tier under a panel that could already carry up to twelve POI rows put St Ives' third tier row 2.5 mm inside the footer plate — painted, then covered, the same failure mode as the terminus lozenges above but on the internal sheet's Key instead of the external sheet's spokes. `KROW_FIT` compresses the pitch to fit when the total would overrun the plate, floored at 3.6 mm (below that the pictograms themselves start touching), with a stderr warning rather than a silently dropped row. **A row count that used to be small and fixed is exactly the kind of thing that stops being small once a new feature adds rows to it — re-check the arithmetic's assumptions, don't just add the new rows to the loop.**
- **The two ends of a footer device can be anchored from opposite directions and drift apart without either end being "wrong" on its own.** `sheetQr`'s code is bottom-anchored (it locks to the last footer line and grows upward as it gets bigger); `sheetUrl`'s text line was top-anchored (a fixed offset above the attribution notes). At 14 mm the two devices only met at the TOP, so the address sat level with the top edge of the code with the whole lower two-thirds of the band beside it empty — visible immediately to a human looking at the sheet, invisible to every byte gate because nothing was overlapping or off-page. Peter caught it by eye mid-session; no automated check would have. Fixed by centring the URL line on the code's vertical midpoint instead of deriving it from the notes' position. **When two page devices are meant to read as one unit, check they're measured from the same reference point, not just that neither one individually violates its own margin.**

## Two hand-placed page devices can collide without either one being wrong on its own (2026-08-19)

High Wycombe's internal sheet shipped with its "Also serving..." `mapNotes` block starting at a hardcoded `y` chosen once, by hand, when the Key above it was eleven rows tall and stopped there. Frequency tiers later added three more rows under it — the same class of change the entries above already describe — and nothing re-checked the note's position against the Key's new bottom, because the two live in **different config blocks** (`mapNotes` vs the Key's own row loop) that neither reads the other's extent. The note started 15 mm inside the Key and its last line ended 3 mm under the footer plate, faded to near-invisibility. Every earlier fix in this file was a *derived boundary* going stale (`RECT.y1`, `MY1`, the URL line); this was two *independently positioned* things going stale against each other, which the `mapNotes`-vs-footer-plate guard (`lastLineY > FOOTER_PLATE_TOP - 2`) could not catch because it only checks the note against the page bottom, never against whatever else is drawn above it. **A hand-placed y that was chosen relative to another element's height is a silent dependency on that height — nothing enforces it, and it breaks the next time that element grows, however far away in the codebase the growth comes from.**

The fix followed the style guide's own rule literally: "fewer words or more room — never smaller type." More room came from generalising a pattern that already existed one line above the changed one — the Key's `allotments` row was already conditional on a POI of that category existing; the other ten rows were not, so `Town Hall` sat in High Wycombe's Key with zero Town Hall POIs anywhere on the sheet, and the same defect existed independently on the Beaconsfield Waitrose place map (Museum/Park/Industrial estate/Town Hall, none present). Filtering the whole array by the same rule the twelfth row already used costs nothing on a town that happens to use every category (it's a no-op — proven by re-running the byte gate against all 8 towns + 5 places + St Ives schematic/diagram after the change: only St Ives stayed byte-identical, because it's the only sheet using all eleven categories, and every other diff was confirmed by hand to be exactly one Key row disappearing with everything below it shifting up — nothing else moved). Fewer words came from re-authoring the note's own text tighter against the engine's *actual* wrap formula (`maxChars = floor((294-6-x)/(size*0.52))`, not a guessed page width) — the original hand-wrapped lines were well short of that limit (58–63 chars used against a true ~67-char budget), so simply re-wrapping the same prose more tightly would have clawed back real room before any content needed cutting at all; worth checking before assuming a hand-wrapped block is already packed.

**`node gen_internal.js > file.svg` is not how these generators are invoked, and using it silently corrupts the output on a timing-dependent basis.** Both `gen_internal.js` and `gen_external_radial.js` write their own SVG via `fs.writeFileSync(DIR+'/….svg', s)` and *then* `console.log()` a one-line summary — the doc's own step 4 ("`node gen_internal.js` → `internal.svg`") describes that as a side effect, not a shell redirection target. Redirecting stdout to the same filename races the shell's own truncate-on-open against the script's independent `fs.writeFileSync` call to that path; this session it corrupted `external.svg` (the trailing `console.log` text landed inside the opening `<svg>` tag) while the same pattern on `internal.svg`, run moments earlier, happened not to — pure timing, not a difference in the two files. **Always run the generators with stdout left alone** (redirect only stderr if you want the warnings on file) and let their own `fs.writeFileSync` produce the SVG; if a render then fails with an XML parse error, check for this before anything else.

## A "straight copy" is only safe for a file nothing downstream measures against (2026-08-19)

March's schematic sheet shipped in the P1 round with its river label refused outright — `labelPos:"auto"` reported "no spot along its drawn ink is both clear of other ink and big enough for the name", on a label as short as `"R. Nene"`. Shortening the text (the guard's own first suggested remedy) changed nothing, because the text was never the problem: the river's own path in the shipped `internal-schematic.svg` had y-coordinates around **-336,825**, hundreds of thousands of units outside the 0–210 mm page frame. The auto-placer was correctly reporting zero usable ink, because there was none anywhere near the visible page.

**Root cause: `schematize_internal.js` warps every linear feature it knows about — except the one legacy path it doesn't.** `features_geo.json` (the modern per-town `features[]` mechanism) gets the full treatment — Douglas-Peucker simplify then `INV(warp(...))` per point, same as roads — but `river_geo.json` (the pre-`features[]` single-river fallback `gen_internal.js` synthesises for a town with no `features[]` of its own, per its own comment: "BACKWARD-COMPAT... so existing towns (St Ives, March) stay byte-identical") was in the **straight-copy list** alongside `atco2name.json`/`routes_intown_atco.json`/`intown_cfg.json` — files that are genuinely position-independent (name lookups, stop lists). `river_geo.json` is not: it is raw `[lat,lon]` polyline data, geometrically identical in shape to one `features_geo.json` entry, and `gen_internal.js`'s legacy fallback (see gen_internal.js's own FEATURES synthesis) reads it directly and draws it as page-mm coordinates without ever knowing it came from a workspace that warped everything else. **March was the only town combining the legacy river fallback with `internalSchematic`**, so this exact combination had never been exercised before — every other schematic town has a real `features[]` array and takes the warped `features_geo.json` path instead.

**The fix** moves `river_geo.json` out of the straight-copy list into its own block, applying the *identical* simplify+warp treatment `features_geo.json` gets, writing back the same array-of-segments shape the file already has (not wrapped in a `{key: [...]}` object, since it's the legacy single-river format). Proven safe on every other sheet the shared file draws before shipping March specifically (the project's standing rule for any change to a file 8 towns + 5 places share): `status.js` showed byte-identical PASS on every other town/place's schematic both before and after, because the new code path is a no-op wherever `river_geo.json` is absent, and inert wherever it's present but unused (every other town takes the `features[]` branch in `gen_internal.js`, which never reads `river_geo.json` at all).

**A file's "straight copy" safety is a claim about every consumer, not about the file's own shape.** `atco2name.json` etc. really are position-independent — nothing downstream measures a coordinate out of them. `river_geo.json` looks equally inert from inside `schematize_internal.js` (which never reads its own copy back), but a *different* file consumer three degrees of separation away — `gen_internal.js`, run afterward, inside the very workspace this script just built — reads it expecting warped coordinates like everything else in that workspace, and had no way to know this one file was still raw. When a workspace is built to hold one consistent coordinate system, "copy this file unchanged" is a claim that either nothing downstream reads it as a coordinate, or that its un-warped coordinates are also its correct ones — check which, don't assume either from the filename.

## A recurring alarm can be the checker's fault, and it cost a month (2026-08-20)

St Neots' monthly refresh report flagged `[WITHDRAWN?] 69 — shipped but gone from BODS` every month from 2026-07. Route 69 was running normally the whole time: 10 journeys/week in that day's feed, listed on bustimes' Eynesbury page and on Stephensons' own site. The fault was in `_gtfs/town_prefixes.json`, which registered the town by prefix `0500HSTNS` alone — and 69 terminates on Eynesbury-coded stops (`0500HEYNE`), so the diff structurally could not see it. Fixed by re-registering as `near [52.22758,-0.27038,4]`, after which the report reads "only expected community gaps".

**This is the mirror image of the Beaconsfield / High Wycombe trap already in that file**, where an over-broad NaPTAN block reported ~30 unrelated routes as `[ADD?]`. Too narrow and too broad both produce a report that is confidently wrong. **A recurring false alarm is worse than a missing one**, because it trains you to skim past the section that will one day carry a real withdrawal. The town's own `verified-services.json` had recorded this exact trap in July — the monthly checker was simply never taught it, so when a build writes a note like that, ask whether the *tooling* needs the same fact.

**When a refresh report flags a shipped route as withdrawn, confirm against a second source before touching the map data, and if it is a false positive fix the registration rather than the town.**

## Three ways a fresh GTFS build lies to you (2026-08-20)

All three hit in one session, and none produces an error:

- **BODS rewrites every `calendar.start_date` to the feed's own start date.** A newly downloaded feed therefore makes the *entire* network look newly registered, and `gtfs_upcoming.py` duly reports every service as "already running; registered to start today (0d away) -> timetable changes then". Seven St Neots services reported that way and not one had changed. Confirm by diffing the old and new databases: if every route moved its start date together and the journey counts are unchanged, it is the feed's stamp, not a timetable change.
- **`gtfs_build.py` writes `feed_info_<dbstem>.json`, named after the OUTPUT file.** Build to a temporary name (`cambridgeshire_new.sqlite`) and you get `feed_info_cambridgeshire_new.json`, while the real `feed_info_cambridgeshire.json` stays stale — so the next report prints a build date weeks older than the data it just mined, with no warning. Move the metadata file when you move the sqlite.
- **A prefix-only pull can miss a route the `--near` pull finds** (see the section above). For a town on a locality boundary, run both and diff them.

## The frequency-tier fields are not called what you would guess (2026-08-20)

Re-deriving the tiers to check a refresh has not moved any line weight, the obvious `medianGapMin` does not exist in `gtfs-services.json`. The real fields are **`coreHeadwayMinutes`** (the median gap in the core of the day, the ≤30 min test for Frequent) and **`longestDaytimeGap`** (the ≤150 min hole test for All-day), alongside `firstDeparture` / `lastDeparture` for the spans-the-day test. Reading a field that does not exist returns `undefined`, every route falls through to the `limited` branch, and the result is a tidy table declaring that four of eight tiers just changed. **A uniform verdict across every row is the tell that the checker is broken, not the data** — the same lesson as the batch script that reported `0/11 published` when all eleven had.

## An area map's generators must travel with it, and the fixture hides that they don't (2026-08-20)

`npm run deliver` for an area map failed its host pre-flight with `gen_internal.js not found (looked in run dir / SKILL_ASSETS / script dir)`. The portal's contract is explicit in `import-map.mjs`'s header: **an area `--src` must contain `gen_internal.js` and `gen_external.js`** (place maps get `engine/place/` copied in for them; area maps supply their own). But this skill **stopped copying a generator into S3/S4 on 2026-08-04** — `routes.json`'s `engine` field records provenance instead — so a modern `S5-render` folder holds no `.js` at all.

**The byte-identical fixture conceals this entirely.** `Areas/_portal-fixture/St Ives` is a pre-2026-08-04 snapshot that still carries `gen_internal.js`, `gen_external.js`, `schematize_internal.js` and `diagram_internal.js`, so `npm run verify` stays green while every real area delivery fails. Until the portal vendors the area engine the way it already vendors the place engine, **copy `gen_internal.js` and the town's external generator (renamed to `gen_external.js`) into the S5 dir before delivering**, and check them byte-identical against the fixture's copies first so you are shipping the generator that actually produced those SVGs. `Areas/**/S5-render/**` is gitignored, so this does not persist — expect to redo it every delivery.

## A missing file is a symptom, not a diagnosis (2026-08-20)

The delivery failure above was first reported as "a host-side engine gap — `/app/engine/expert/` is missing `gen_internal.js`", and that reading survived several *confirming* observations: the file really is absent from that directory, and the expert generators really do exit 1 without it. It was wrong, and it would have sent someone to change the deployed image. The question never asked was **which side of the contract was supposed to provide the file** — `import-map.mjs` answers it in its own header, and every live map's data dir on the host already carried a copy, which should have been the clue. **Before proposing a fix to where a file is, find out where it was meant to come from.**

## Normalising an angle is not free — the same bearing is not the same float (2026-08-21)

`design.fixedOrientation` accepts any number of degrees, and the obvious tidiness is to normalise it into 0–360 on the way in, so `-66` and `294` both land on `294` and the config reads consistently. **Do not.** They are the same bearing to a reader and *not* the same number to the FPU: `Math.cos(-66 * π/180)` is `0.4067366430758002` and `Math.cos(294 * π/180)` is `0.40673664307580054`, a difference in the last bits that propagates through every projected coordinate on the sheet.

The consequences are exactly the ones that are hardest to spot. A sheet pinned with `fixedOrientation: -66` would stop being byte-identical to the same sheet built from the equivalent `internalRoads.rotationDeg: -66`, so the byte-identical reproduce gate would flag a "regression" that is nothing of the kind. Worse, `freeze_orientation.js` reads the applied angle out of `build-meta.json` and writes it back into `routes.json` — so a capture→write→rebuild round trip, whose entire purpose is to leave the map exactly where it is, would have moved it very slightly every time it ran.

Caught by testing the round trip rather than by reading the code: build with `internalRoads.rotationDeg: -66`, keep the bytes, rebuild with `design.fixedOrientation: -66`, and compare. Byte-identical once the normalisation was removed. **The general rule: any "harmless" tidying of a value that feeds floating-point geometry can move the artwork, so leave the author's number alone and let the trig deal with it.**

## build_log.js protects the skill's builds and nothing else (2026-08-21)

`rollout.js` and `rollout_places.js` capture the generators' stderr, classify it through `build_log.js` and stop `--apply` on a BLOCKING warning. **Nothing on the portal side does any of this.** `community-bus-maps`' `src/render/renderMap.js` reads `res.stderr` **only when the generator exits non-zero**, and the refusing guards exit 0 — so on the success path it discards the whole stream unread. That matters because the portal **re-renders** when a delivered map is imported or a proposed update is accepted, so the bytes that actually reach the public are produced by a path with no warning capture at all. The customer's live editor preview is the same.

So a sheet can pass every portal gate while carrying a label the engine refused to draw, and the only record of it is a `build-warnings.txt` in the S4 run folder on the laptop — which the portal never sees.

**Do not read this as "the vendored copies have drifted".** They have not: `renderMap.js` is portal-only and `build_log.js` is skill-only, so `status.js` reports every vendored row in sync and is correct. What diverged is the *behaviour* on the two sides of the boundary, which no file comparison can express — worth remembering before trusting a content-based drift check to catch this class of thing.

The proposed fix is to make the blocking guards exit non-zero behind `STRICT_GUARDS=1`, so every spawn path catches them through the error handling it already has, rather than each caller re-deriving severity by parsing English. Written up with the evidence and the falsification plan in `buses-data`'s `Development Docs/stderr-guard-capture-plan_2026-08-21.md`. **Not built as at 2026-08-21.**
